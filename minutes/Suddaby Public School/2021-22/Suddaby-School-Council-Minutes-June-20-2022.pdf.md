## Administrators’ Report

Changes to Outdoor Supervision - 22/23 School Year:  
We have been working through the process of setting up our supervision schedule for next year.  
Here are a few changes we’re working through, with feedback from staff:  
\1) We are going to have all of the school out at once - We will all eat first (11:00 am to  
11:20 am and 1:40 pm to 2:00 pm) and then go outside (11:20 am to 11:40 am and 2:00  
pm to 2:20 pm).  
\2) We are going to use the front of the school so our creative playground and a portion of  
the front yard will be available for students to play.  
\3) We will restrict the use of part of the tarmac - Tarmac close to the Lancaster door and  
bike racks will be restricted so that we can safely supervise the rest of the yard.  
Many things will stay consistent, including:  
\1) The use of the fields and gaga ball pit (depending on weather, etc.).  
\2) The use of most of the tarmac at the back of the school.  
We met with our staffing committee and discussed our morning rain weather policy. After  
reviewing past policies, the educators felt strongly that our current practice of not having an  
early rain bell is best for student safety. Staff expressed concerns about other solutions,  
including safely supervising students, especially our kindergarten friends, and effectively  
monitoring who is within the school.

## Pizza Days

We have finished our 6 weeks of pizza day for this school year. The celebrations and challenges  
(as described at our May meeting) remained similar throughout.  
–Positives:  
\1) More slices of pizza were ordered than the last pizza day - 435 slices vs. 353 slices  
(February 2020). Stars ordered all of the options we had available, including Halal  
pepperoni, a gluten-free crust, and dairy-free cheese. We had Stars donate additional  
money to cover the cost, and we had Stars receive pizza at no cost. Overall, the  
feedback on the taste of the pizza was positive.  
\2) Pizza delivery did go more smoothly as the weeks went on and we developed an  
additional list of students who hadn’t ordered pizza at first but were able to receive it for  
free after we received caregiver permission.  
Challenges:  
\1) The wide variety of options made distribution more difficult. We did have some Stars  
who were disappointed because they thought that they were going to receive pizza. This  
could have been due to confusion with SchoolDay, forms, etc.  
Going forward:  
\1) How could we improve distribution? Should we remove options? How can we make the  
initial setup more sustainable?  
Thank-yous: Huge thank yous to Lisa Blundon, Katha Broschek, Colin Read and the students  
from room 24. Thanks also to the educators who helped to hand out pizza in classrooms.  
Without their contributions, pizza day simply would not have been possible.  
Scott Norton  
We were pleased to welcome Scott Norton from The Healing of the Seven Generations to  
Suddaby. His presentation to caregivers was well attended overall and many provided positive  
feedback. Students seemed very engaged during his visits. They had many questions for Scott  
that they shared. We were able to meet outside and the size of the workshops allowed for a lot  
of student participation overall. We are hopeful that these visits are the first step into more  
partnerships with Scott and The Healing of the Seven Generations.

## Other Issues - End of year BBQ

On June 22nd, Council was pleased to be able to host a BBQ for all Suddaby students and staff  
to celebrate the end of the year. A variety of hot dogs, chips, and juice boxes were offered to all  
students and staff at no cost, and outdoor games were played in the back playground for part of  
the afternoon. We wish to thank Mr Vanderpool, our volunteers, the teachers and the grade 6  
students who helped out.  

## Other Issues - Strongstart

Strongstart this year faced several challenges, including a very late start due to COVID, and a  
drop in available volunteers due to both COVID and changes to the requirements to volunteer in  
the school. However, 4 children were enrolled in the program, and 3 completed it with 162  
measured gains.
