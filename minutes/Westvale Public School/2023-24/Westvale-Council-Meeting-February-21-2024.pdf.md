## Principal’s Report – Mary Sue

• The K1 teacher is back from leave.  
• In February, Macourt Puppeteers are doing fairy tales with primary and kindergarten students.  
• It should be noted that while vaccine reminders go out via the school, it is between families and  
public health. If the vaccinations are not satisfied per public health then public health issues the  
suspension. The principal cannot override this, however you can talk to public health. For more  
information please see www.regionofwaterloo.ca/vaccines  
• Pink shirt day is Feb 28th and is for being kind and to support anti-bullying. On pink shirt day, we  
will also be running a fundraiser for nutrition for learning and will be selling friendship bracelets  
at a cost of \$2 each or 3 for \$5.  
• There is a multi year plan for literacy (https://www.wrdsb.ca/blog/2024/02/12/announcing-the-  
wrdsb-structured-literacy-multi-year-plan/)  
o The new curriculum teaches phonetic awareness and decoding vs 3-cueing method  
o We are currently in year 2, and this applies across all grade levels for students (at a  
grade-appropriate level)  
• Community report card is available on the Board website, which includes the progress of all  
board initiatives.  
o This report card is available in multiple languages.  
o Shows the graduation rate out of high school, which is the main goal.  
￼  
o Centering students is another goal (involving students in the processes and checking on  
how safe they feel, etc.)  
o Equitable outcomes is another goal (e.g. making sure grade 9-12 have one to one  
chrome book access, and that there are accessibility upgrades in schools)  
o There are also goals for connection, collaboration, and support for well being  
• Allison Yeung (thesmartphoneeffect) would like to come speak to council or do an information  
night for parents on her recommendations on Smart Phone usage for kids  
o Impact of social media on children is main topic  
o Objectives:  
▪ Describe the scope of the problem with social media and video games, as it  
relates to the mental health of children and teens  
▪ Understand the factors that have contributed to screentime overuse  
▪ Understand the role of dopamine in causing addition, and specifically how levels  
of dopamine are influenced by social media  
▪ Understand how to approach a child asking for a smart phone  
▪ Understand when a child is ready for a smart phone/social media  
▪ Understand boundaries to help reduce harm from screens  
• Jacob Hespler will no longer be running before and after school care as of next school year; it  
will be offered instead by board employees (e.g. ECEs).
