## WRAPS-C MEETING UPDATE – A. Strickland

## April meeting update

○ Guest speaker from Transportation, discussed safe alternate dropoff locations, similar to the  
presentation given to Clemens Council a few months ago  
■ Parking lot barriers at Clemens have made the parking lot safer, Kiss & Drop process is  
working well. Drive to 5 approach is not running yet, but can be considered for next year.  
○ Equity and Inclusion Officer discussed the dress code policy. Currently the policy is very open and  
leads to different interpretations across schools, goal is to have all schools following same  
approach. Rules need to be considered with different cultural traditions. Rules also seem to be  
more heavily weighted towards females. New policy will look to encourage students to wear what  
they feel comfortable in, not allow undergarments to be substituted as outerwear, avoid promotion  
of any violent/criminal/harmful activities.  
■ Mr Tiwari shared that the approach at Clemens is not to teach girls to be ashamed of their  
bodies, but to teach all students how to be respectful.  
■ General approach is that if parents/guardians are happy, then school admin is happy,  
within reason.  
○ Small group discussions about PIC. Focus on parent volunteering in school, but was premature as  
schools are just starting to open up again.  

## UNLEARN MURALS– C. Wilson

● Murals were installed over March break and were chosen by student votes. They look great!  
● UNLEARN community sessions range from \$1000 (1hr) to \$1900 (3hr). 75 min session is recommended  
(\$1150 plus 10% off). Timing for a session would be good now to tie it in to the new murals, but could also  
consider September when perhaps we could open it up in person. No cap on participants, could also be  
open to community members beyond student families. Mr. Tiwari would opt for the fall, use it as a  
community open house, perhaps tie to Meet The Teacher night and a food truck event.  
● Question whether we have any diversity issues apparent at the school. Biases can come from home, but  
students are very open in recent years to challenging biases. It cannot be said that there are no issues, but  
students are being respectful, owning it when a mistake happens, and classrooms have homegrown respect  
rules that are helping to promote positivity.
