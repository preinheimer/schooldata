## Students as Writers Project Proposal

Presenter: Taru and Andrew Cairns  
• There was a presentation given on the proposal. There was a brief discussion about the  
proposal how students are going to write the books in dual languages.  
• This is going to be run like a class project where all the students individually or in pairs  
will be creating fiction or non-fiction text based on their cultural heritage or their own  
stories.  
• Parent Volunteer involvement is to be planned for the corrections in the first languages  
is recommended as one parent had an experience with students using Google translator  
for the language translation.  
• Student should know how to speak or write to choose the language.  
• This is going to be a pilot project for the whole next academic year starting from Fall,  
target is to complete the printing and give the books to students by May.  
• Each book is printed 3 copies, one for student, class and school libraries.  
• Some of the benefits from the project were presented and requested for an amount of  
360\$ for this pilot project.  
• Council voted on the item (85.7% - YES; 14.3% - NO) and approved the amount and it  
should be updated in the treasurer report.  
• There was suggestion to let know about the project to all the students to create interest  
in other students.

## Teachers’ Report for School Council

Wednesday, May 17, 2023  
The latest and greatest at Abraham Erb!  
Room 28 participated in an invasive plant species removal activity in White Cedar Forest. We  
met with a volunteer from the City of Waterloo and learned about the problem with invasive  
plant species (in particular Garlic Mustard) and how to successfully get rid of it. We pulled  
about four-yard bags full of Garlic Mustard out of the forest! We were rewarded with time well  
spent on a beautiful day in the forest and a freezie for the walk back to school.  
\~Miss. Fox  
Room 10 has studied volume and capacity by borrowing sand and water tables from the  
kindergarten rooms. We measured the length of dinosaurs on small cards, through toys and  
then measured the real length of dinosaurs using meter sticks in the hallway! The Barosaurus  
(48m) went from the fire doors by the kindergarten room - all the way to pod 1! Loads of  
fun! We are also in the midst of making a paper mini-town to show important aspects of  
communities, including the things that people need and important community helpers. We are  
looking forward to our Tim Horton’s center to study coins and money!  
\~Ms Watson  
The Running Club has started for junior students! Mr. Moffet has organized an amazing  
program where students are playing games while getting lots of exercise. This is a great way for  
students to practice for Track & Field while having fun with friends after school. The group went  
for their first forest run where they played relay races in small groups. Various teachers, from  
primary and junior, have been out to support this club that takes place twice a week until the  
end of June.  
• Mme McAllister  
Ms. Varady, Mr. Pool and Mme McAllister’s class had a fantastic visit from Wild Ontario. This  
organization is an environmental and science education program in the Department of  
Integrative Biology at the University of Guelph. Students had the opportunity to see a red-tailed  
hawk, an American kestrel, and a great horned owl up close!  
• Mme McAllister  

## Principal’s Report for School Council

Wednesday, May 17th, 2023  
School  
• In partnership with Student Transportation Services of Waterloo Region (STSWR), we  
will be having a “bike to school week” from May 29th to June 2nd. We will be doing daily  
announcements to encourage students to ride their bikes to school and doing a “count”  
of bikes in the racks to submit to STSWR for a chance to win a prize for our school (a  
new bike rack).  
• Next year, we will be making a small change to our daily schedule. School start and end  
times will remain the same (9:10 a.m. and 3:30 p.m.) We will be having 4 periods of  
instruction, followed by 1st NB, followed by another 4 periods of instruction, followed by  
2nd NB, followed by 2 periods of instruction at the end of the day. Currently, we are  
following a 4-3-3 model. The rationale behind this is to increase the instructional blocks  
when students are at their freshest and best able to learn. It will also create the  
opportunity for more double periods of instruction. This will shift the time of our  
second nutrition break to 1:50 – 2:30 (currently it is 1:20 – 2:00). Our website will be  
updated accordingly.  
• Our Art Show and Book Swap on April 27th was a huge success! The event was very well  
attended and lots of positive feedback was received. Personally, I think this would be an  
excellent even to run on a yearly basis.  
• We will be having a Jump Rope for Heart event day on June 19th where all students will  
participate in a variety of fun activities, including skipping during the second block of the  
day. On May 26th, we will be having a Jump Rope for Heart assembly for all the students  
to talk about the event and the option of students being able to do some fundraising for  
the Heart and Stroke Foundation. Donations will be accepted online and also in cash for  
any families interested in participating in the fundraising portion of this event.  
• This is a very busy time of year with many events on the horizon. Our Volunteer  
Breakfast is being held the morning of May 25th in our library from 8 – 9 a.m. Our  
Kindergarten Open House is the evening of June 12th from 6 – 7 p.m. Our Junior Track  
and Field meet will be held on May 30th and the Regional Track and Field meet is at  
Jacob Hespeler Secondary School on June 21st. All of our grade 3 and 6 students will be  
participating in EQAO testing the week of June 5th  
– June 9th. Grades 6s are going to  
Camp Ki-way on May 18th and 19th and will also be visiting Laurelwood PS on Jun 19th for  
an orientation.  
Board  
• Two of our WRDSB elementary school have now officially been renamed. Ryerson PS in  
Cambridge is now called Blue Heron PS. A.R. Kaufman PS in Kitchener is now called  
Hillside PS. The school renaming process is a part of our commitment to making  
concrete changes in support of the Calls to Action outlined by the Truth and  
Reconciliation Commission. The WRDSB began the renaming process for both schools in  
early 2023. The work was led by School Renaming Committees, made up of trustees,  
school staff, board staff, parents & caregivers, students, community members,  
Indigenous community members and representatives from community organizations.  
• The Waterloo Educational Foundation Incorporated (WEFI) has renewed their  
commitment to funding local organizations that support student nutrition programs.  
The WEFI Board of Directors passed a motion to provide \$30,000 to be distributed  
equally to our community partners Food4Kids Waterloo Region, the May Court Club of  
Kitchener-Waterloo and Nutrition for Learning.  
• Two WRDSB trustee positions are now vacant. Sadly, Trustee Fred Meissner passed  
away suddenly last week. All our school flags will be flying at half-mast until the day of  
his funeral. Trustee Marie Snyder has also recently resigned her position due to health  
reasons.  
Province  
• The Ministry of Education has recently announced that they will be updating the Grade  
10 Career Studies curriculum to include mental health literacy and is introducing  
mandatory resources for teachers and students on mental health literacy in Grades 7  
and 8. The Ministry will also be providing funding to further students’ access to school-  
based mental health professional and services year round, along with helping Boards  
meet local needs and priorities related to mental health, including professional  
development, student engagement, wellness promotion. We will have to wait for more  
details on what this will look like for us in the WRDSB.
