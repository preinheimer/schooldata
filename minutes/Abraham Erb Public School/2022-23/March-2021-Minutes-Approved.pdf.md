## Math Carriculum

Presenter: Mrs. Wentworth  
▪ PK: Welcome to Meghan Wentworth a teacher at Abraham Erb who will be discussing the new  
math curriculum.  
o Mrs. Wentworth is currently teaching grade 6 for the Distance Learning Program and has a  
large group of students from Abraham Erb.  
o She started her career at Abraham Erb as an EA and then moved into the role of a teacher  
in 2015. After a brief stint at another WRDSB school, she returned in 2018.  
o She has been an integral part of the ABE ERB team, especially with all things technology:  
technological support, technology deployment plan, new technology management.  
o She has also been a leader in supporting school-wide event planning such as graduation.  
▪ Teachers are still getting used to the new math curriculum and are all still learning. The new  
curriculum is as of 2020 (last revised in 2005).  
o Was created after consultation in 2018 to help improve student achievement.  
o Part of a 4 year plan to help improve performance in math, help students solve everyday  
math problems, and increase student employability for the jobs of tomorrow.  
▪ Focus is on social-emotional learning, numbers, algebra, data, spatial sense, and financial literacy.  
The social-emotional learning strand goes through all the other 5 strands.  
o Note that the social-emotional piece is paused in distance learning as it is unclear how to  
evaluate and incorporate it into the program at this time.  
o More time on new and key concepts, more user-friendly language. There are fewer  
expectations and the focus is on key concepts.  
o Real-life applications: Teachers are given sample problems to give to the students.  
o There is a new focus on coding, even in kindergarten.  
▪ Social-emotional learning is about:  
o How to approach concepts in a positive manner  
o Helping kids with anxiety around math  
o Focus on general facts to increase overall math confidence.  
▪ Note that the things taught in the old curriculum are still part of the new curriculum. It’s just been  
reorganized.  
▪ The new curriculum has only one mark for math, while the old one had a mark for each strand.  
1  
▪ The new curriculum has a heavy emphasis on number facts (e.g. addition, subtraction, fractions,  
etc.), solving real-world problems, and using different models and tools to solve. Trying to show  
many different tools as students learn differently.  
▪ New this year, teachers across Ontario were all given a sample long-range plan to follow. This has  
been very helpful to the teachers as they have a clear plan that spans the year and is consistent  
throughout the province.  
▪ Can find full documentation online by searching Ontario 2020 Math Curriculum. Broken down by  
grade so can easily see the new expectations.  
▪ Questions:  
o VK - Please explain about what the social-emotional piece means.  
▪ MW - Helping kids develop a positive growth mindset, address anxiety they might  
have about math, and help them break down problems into smaller ones and  
encouraging them to persevere and get through it. The evaluation is all  
observational, there’s no specific grade.  
o VK - How can you report on each individual strengths and weaknesses if it’s combined into  
one mark?  
▪ MW - Teachers will add this information in the comments section. The social-  
emotional piece will also be addressed in the comments section on the report card.  
o JK - How do you determine what tools to use?  
▪ MW - Tools are now suggested in the long-range plan given by the government.  
These recommended tools are very helpful. In the past, teachers would need to  
research this themselves. That said, teachers can (and still) do look for ideas on  
their own.  
o FH - Why has the assessment been changed to one mark for all math?  
▪ MW - Not sure. Might be because we want to look at math as a whole picture  
instead of breaking it down by subject. Parents should focus less on the mark itself  
and look to the comments to see their child’s strengths and areas for growth.  
o FH - Where does coding fall in the curriculum?  
▪ MW - Coding fits in the Data strand.  
o VK - Is there something for parents and volunteers so we can provide help in a meaningful  
way?  
▪ MW - Read through the documentation online. The expectations are quite clear,  
particularly from a non-teacher lens. Also, it’s always helpful to check in with the  
teacher.  
▪ PK - Long-range plans have provided consistency across the schools and boards.  
▪ PK - This is the first year of us exploring the expectations for the new math curriculum. Note that  
there has not been professional development that would normally take place due to Covid.  
Teachers have a broad understanding, but training has not yet been provided.  
o PK - Note that the expectations and marking guidelines are provided by the government,  
not at the school board level.
