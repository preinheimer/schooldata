## Welcome / Territory Acknowledgment

As we gather virtually this evening, we would like to honor the existence of the First  
Peoples by acknowledging that we are on the traditional land of the Anishinaabe,  
Haudenosaunee, and Mississauga peoples. New Dundee Public School occupies the  
land of the Between the Lakes Treaty from 1792. As learners, we continue to  
understand this place has significance to the First Peoples because of the rolling hills,  
forests, flat land, and the proximity to the Nith and Grand River, which is fed by the  
Great Lakes. Settlers need to take responsibility to continue our education and honor of  
the First Peoples. Let’s work to honor the Dish with One Spoon covenant, a peace  
agreement made between the Indigenous nations before the settlers arrived. It  
characterizes our collective responsibility to each other and to Mother Earth. We should  
only take what we need and leave enough for future generations.

● Asbestos update - Response from an Environmental Officer at WRDSB:  
● Asbestos is a fibrous mineral that was used extensively in Ontario from about  
1900 until the 1980’s in the manufacture of construction and industrial products,  
mostly due to its characteristics of strength, heat resistance and chemical  
resistance. Asbestos Containing Materials (ACM) were also commonly used in  
building materials such as pipe and boiler insulation, texture finishes, floor and  
ceiling tiles, and spray applied for fireproofing and thermal insulation.  
● Asbestos fibres are a concern when they become airborne as a result of  
disturbance or deterioration. It is acknowledged that all ACM do not have to be  
removed or replaced to ensure a safe working environment, but proper control  
measures and procedures are required to prevent the disturbance of ACM and to  
protect maintenance staff and building occupants from exposure to harmful levels  
of airborne asbestos.  
● Under Ontario Regulation 278/05, the WRDSB is required to provide  
documentation pertaining to ACM within its buildings.  
● A consultant had inspected / surveyed each school and provided an Asbestos  
Audit Report identifying the type and locations of known and suspected ACM.  
The inspections were performed with good intent and purpose to determine the  
location and condition of ACM. These inspections were non-invasive, whereby  
ACM that may be visually concealed by, but not necessarily limited to ceilings,  
walls, bulkheads, floors, roofs, vertical chases, trenches, crawl spaces, shafts,  
tunnels and other similar structures was not identified. However, ACM that could  
be present in these locations may become apparent during future construction,  
renovation, demolition, and/or maintenance activities.  
● Annually, a consultant is inspecting / surveying each school to re-assess and  
document the condition of identified ACM present within the building and make  
appropriate recommendations for management, abatement or remedial activities,  
as required.  
● An Asbestos Binder is located in the Main Office of each school, in a visible  
and accessible location. The following documents are in the binder:  
● -Asbestos Audit Update Report - completed every few years, after renovations /  
additions  
● -Asbestos Inspection - completed every year;  
● -Confirmation of Asbestos Removal Letter(s) - ACM was removed from a location  
due to recommendation from an inspection or school improvement  
● -Lab Results for Additional Sampling  
● During the last few years, the Board has removed a large quantity of ACM  
(floors, pipe insulation and fittings) at New Dundee PS, but there are still  
some identified and will be removed during renovations (windows and  
doors sealant, pipe insulation and fittings in concealed locations).  
● The other important thing to note is during our ACM audits, one of the main  
components is the condition of the ACM. If there is any sign of  
deterioration, it is removed by certified Asbestos removal contractors right  
away.

## Care Committee (Kelly)

Dedicated to supporting the well-being of members through acts of kindness, empathy,  
and practical assistance. The committee's primary goal is to foster a compassionate  
environment where people feel cared for and supported during times of need, whether  
due to celebrations or personal challenges. Coordinating gestures: Sending cards,  
flowers, or small gifts to show care and support during life events and calendar  
celebrations. Collecting donations from the community is part of this role as it can not be  
covered by school council funds.
