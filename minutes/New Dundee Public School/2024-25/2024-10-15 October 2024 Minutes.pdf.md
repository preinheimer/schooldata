## Welcome / Territory Acknowledgment

As we gather virtually this evening, we would like to honor the existence of the First Peoples by  
acknowledging that we are on the traditional land of the Anishinaabe, Haudenosaunee, and  
Mississauga peoples. New Dundee Public School occupies the land of the Between the Lakes  
Treaty from 1792. As learners, we continue to understand this place has significance to the First  
Peoples because of the rolling hills, forests, flat land, and the proximity to the Nith and Grand  
River, which is fed by the Great Lakes. Settlers need to take responsibility to continue our  
education and honor of the First Peoples. Let’s work to honor the Dish with One Spoon  
covenant, a peace agreement made between the Indigenous nations before the settlers arrived.  
It characterizes our collective responsibility to each other and to Mother Earth. We should only  
take what we need and leave enough for future generations.

## Playground Structure Update

● All three play structures were inspected the week of September 30th and no  
repairs were required (passed the inspection). Mr. Bell reached out to the  
Facilities Manager, who then reached out to a maintenance foreperson in Facility  
Services to determine the lifespan of the structures. From a 2023 inspection, it  
was determined that the life expectancy of the structures is 5-10 years. The  
Kindergarten play structure is quite new. The red “Junior” play structure is  
approximately 14 years old. The oldest play structure that is shaped like a tractor  
is a classic playground that is approximately 24 years old. The Facilities  
Department is receiving many questions about the lifespan of play structures,  
and is currently working to determine how to support this process.  
● WRAPSC said to start a school council budget line for play structure replacement  
● Process to replace or build playground is enormous and has to go through union  
approved contractors

## Care Committee

Dedicated to supporting the well-being of members through acts of kindness, empathy, and  
practical assistance. The committee's primary goal is to foster a compassionate environment  
where people feel cared for and supported during times of need, whether due to celebrations or  
personal challenges. Coordinating gestures: Sending cards, flowers, or small gifts to show care  
and support during life events and calendar celebrations. Collecting donations from the  
community is part of this role as it can not be covered by school council funds.
