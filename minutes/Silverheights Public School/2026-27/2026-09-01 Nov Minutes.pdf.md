Board policy. School councils provide an opportunity and the means to advise the principal and the

management of the day-to-day operation of the school rests with the principal.

Letter to be sent to Trustees. Advocating communication from schools  
Dear WRDSB Trustees,  
I am writing on behalf of WRAPSC to advocate for the well-being of our students. We believe it is crucial  
for caregivers to receive communication from principals and teachers when an incident occurs in front of  
their child, whether it happens in the classroom or elsewhere on school property. While we understand  
and respect the need for privacy for those directly involved in these incidents, it is important to  
recognize that the students who witness these events are also impacted. Many of these students are  
witnessing moments of violence or emotional instability, which can lead to a normalization of such  
behavior. As a result, some students are becoming afraid to attend school and are reluctant to speak  
about what they have seen. If caregivers were informed about what their child has witnessed, they  
would be more likely to engage in conversations with their child and provide the necessary support. This  
could include helping their child develop an action plan for coping with such situations in the future,  
thereby reducing fear and anxiety. The communication sent home does not need to go into detail about  
the incident itself but could instead provide tips on how to talk to children about what they’ve  
experienced and how to support both students and staff in these situations. Effective communication  
can only contribute positively to the well-being of our students. We urge you not to use privacy as an  
excuse to withhold such important communication. After all, caregivers regularly receive notifications  
about public health issues like lice without privacy concerns being cited and these situations affect  
student well-being as well. We ask that the WRDSB develop a letter, much like the one used for lice, to  
inform caregivers any time their child has witnessed an incident. Thank you for considering this  
important matter. We believe that by working together, we can create a safer and more supportive  
environment for our students.

## THE CRITICAL NEED

Did you know, an average school program costs \$15,000, with some  
costing as high as \$70,000!? And that funding from the Ministry of  
Children, Community, and Social Services covers less than 30% of our  
region's program funding?  
With over 180 programs in schools across Waterloo region and the highest  
rates of food insecurity in our community's history, we need our  
community's support more than ever. Here are some ways your parent  
council can help.  
LET'S FUNDRAISE!  
Currently, school fundraising accounts for ~7% of our total revenue, but it's  
growing every year! And every dollar counts. A simple \$200 fundraiser can  
help provide as many as 180 meals delivered right to your school.  
Get your school involved and host your own school fundraiser like a 'read-  
a-thon', bake sale, talent show or car wash. No matter the fundraiser, you  
can contact our team to set up a fundraising page to help you raise funds  
for your program.  
GET INVOLVED.  
Participate in our upcoming fundraising events, outlined on Pages 6 & 7!  
Or create one that suits your school's needs. Whether it is a bake sale,  
charity run, or auction, your involvement helps raise vital funds and fosters  
a strong community spirit.  
SPREAD THE WORD.  
If you're not in a position to donate, we challenge you to find a monthly  
donor! Just \$20 a month (2 lattes and muffins!) provides over 200 meals a  
YEAR!  
You can also share our fundraising campaigns with friends, family, and  
colleagues. The more people know, the more support we can gather to  
sustain these essential programs. Follow us on Instagram  
(@nutritionforlearning) and tag us in your school's program and initiatives.

## TOONIE DRIVE

Campaign Dates: Anytime during the 2024-2025 school year!  
One of the simplest and most accessible ways to fundraise is by hosting  
a change drive!  
During your chosen campaign window, print Nutrition for Learning's  
Toonie Drive flyers and send them home with your students. Families,  
neighbours, and/or friends can then tape a toonie, or any extra change,  
to the flyer. Send a message to parents through your virtual platform to  
boost your fundraising results!  
Nutrition for Learning will provide the following materials to make your  
school's Toonie Drive a success!  
• Toonie Drive Flyers - colouring pages for your students to customize  
before taking home! (best printed on heavier weight paper)  
• Fundraising page where donations can be made directly online.  
Social media assets to share with your community!  
• Email copy to be sent through School Day notifying your community  
about the fundraiser!
