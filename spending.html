Big projects & what things cost

Based on WRDSB elementary school council minutes posted online (2018–2026). Part 1 looks at projects costing $50,000+ in total (council + board + grants), at any stage: how long they took to fund, who gave money, and what was built. Part 2 groups everything councils bought for $3,000+ into cost tables. Every project name links to its full entry and minutes.

Part 1: Projects of $50,000+

Takeaways

  • Time: completed playgrounds of $45K–$77K took about 2–3 years from start to install. Raising the money took 1–2 years; board procurement and tendering typically added about a year (Millen Woods' RFP slipped from spring to December 2024; Clemens Mill tendered in 2025 after funding was in hand by late 2024).
  • Money mostly came from recurring council fundraising, not grants: Bingo (lottery licence, ~$18K/yr at Clemens Mill and Elgin), pizza days (~$17K/yr), chocolate campaigns (up to $45K gross at Hespeler), Dance-a-thons and fun fairs, plus WEFI donations with tax receipts.
  • Grants named in the minutes are small: Optimist Club matching up to $5K (Smithson), Charitree $2K (Country Hills outdoor classroom), Walmart $1K (J F Carmichael), and the City of Kitchener's Love My Hood (up to $10K; applied for by J F Carmichael and Groh, results not recorded). Rick Hansen Foundation, TD Friends of the Environment, Costco and Jays Cares were explored. Hespeler's shade-structure grant was rejected because it was oversubscribed. The single largest outside gift was a $10,000 special donation at Suddaby.
  • The board is the biggest outside funder: ~$15K for site prep and removal is standard (Clemens Mill, Cedar Creek, Smithson); it paid 44% at Suddaby ($21K) and the whole ~$120K playground at Howard Robertson as part of a child-care centre build. The Ministry doesn't fund playgrounds, and the board won't replace one that still passes inspection.
  • Board rules shape projects: board-approved vendors only, a purchase order over $3K, three quotes over $10K, a lengthy approval process for playground changes over $10K, and accessibility requirements for new structures. At J F Carmichael, reviewers of the donation letter said a thank-you plaque couldn't be offered (reason not recorded).

Donor recognition seen in other minutes

  • Cedarbrae, courtyard upgrade (Oct 2024): mostly donated through Pioneer Craftsman's "Give Back" project. The school held an official ribbon-cutting ceremony to thank the businesses involved and planned a plaque acknowledging donors. 2024-10-09
  • Board memorial policy (Cedar Creek, Apr 2025): WRDSB has an "Expressions of Sympathy" policy. The board's position is that plaques and benches make school spaces "look like cemeteries" and cause grief if they have to be moved or get damaged. The board instead plants a tree. This was about a staff memorial, not donors, but it may explain the reluctance about plaques. 2025-04-23
  • Other informal approaches: Blue Heron proposed listing donating businesses in a "School Directory" and having students write thank-you cards November; Conestogo publicly thanked Home Hardware in its minutes for a discount on a garden project 2024-09-12.
  • What WRDSB's published rules say (checked Nov 2026):
    • AP 4989 – Partnerships & Sponsorships (Feb 2024): a donation is given "without expectation of any benefit" and gets a WEFI tax receipt, while a sponsorship buys advertising and gets no receipt. Sponsor recognition must be agreed between sponsor and principal (4.11), must be time-limited, and "Indefinite recognitions of a sponsor are not permitted" (5.3). Agreements max out at 5 years (5.5), and signage that is part of a facility's structure isn't allowed (5.7). Listed as unacceptable: "Funds provided to build a gymnasium or library" and a "Scoreboard with the logo of the company who donated it" (6.2). Every sponsorship needs written approval from the board's Superintendent, Business Services (form FS-17-PS).
    • AP 4490 – Waterloo Education Foundation (2007 version): receipts only for gifts benefiting the student body generally, none if the donor gets "direct personal gain". Its only recognition step is that receipts go through the principal "so that a thank-you note can be attached". It says nothing about plaques or naming.
    • Policy 4020 – Naming of Board Facilities (June 2021): schools and facilities can't be named for individuals or "after a corporation".
    • Policy 3005 – Expressions of Sympathy (rev. May 2026): the board marks deaths with a condolence letter and memorial trees placed by grounds staff. It doesn't mention plaques or benches, so the "cemeteries" reasoning in Cedar Creek's minutes isn't in the written policy.
    • 2021 Naming Review: notes that some other boards allow donor naming and plaques, and that WRDSB's donation, sponsorship and naming rules "may require alignment" on donor recognition.
    • None of these documents explicitly bans a simple thank-you plaque for donors. Taken together, though, permanent named recognition for a receipted donation doesn't fit the rules: it would be a "benefit", or indefinite sponsor advertising.
  • Not found: no minutes mention naming rights, donor walls, engraved bricks, or sponsor names or logos on playground equipment or signs.
